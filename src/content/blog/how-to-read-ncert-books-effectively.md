Almost every topper says the same thing: "Read NCERT." Very few explain *how*. Reading a chapter once like a novel does not work. Here is a simple method that does.

## Read 1 — understand (slow)

- Read the chapter with a pencil. Underline definitions, laws and anything in **bold**.
- Stop at every in-text question and try it before reading on.
- For science, redraw every diagram once in rough. You are not making notes yet — you are making sure you understand.

## Read 2 — extract (notes)

- Now make **one page** of notes for the chapter: definitions, formulas, named examples, diagram labels, dates.
- Do every end-of-chapter exercise. Mark the ones you got wrong.
- For Biology and Chemistry, list the "exceptions" and "examples" the book mentions — they are frequent question material.

## Read 3 — revise (fast)

- Read the chapter quickly, checking it against your one-page note. Add anything you missed.
- Re-do only the exercise questions you marked wrong.
- From now on, revise from your notes and go back to the book only for diagrams and tables.

## Small things that make a big difference

- **Don't skip boxes, captions and tables.** Questions are often written from them.
- **Keep the chapter PDF on your phone.** Five-minute reads on the bus add up. Every NCERT chapter is free on our [NCERT PDF page](/ncert-pdf/) — read online or download.
- **Print smart.** If you prefer paper, fit 2 pages per A4 sheet with the [pages-per-sheet tool](/tools/pages-per-sheet/).
